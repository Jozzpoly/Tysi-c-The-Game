# Hand Workspace Stability — Adversarial Review

Status: explicit challenge to `minimum necessary disturbance` and spatial-constancy hypotheses.

## The attractive intuition

Experience Foundation currently values:

- personal hand ordering;
- stable landmarks;
- local rather than global deformation;
- minimum necessary disturbance after state changes.

This is plausible and supported by some spatial-memory/distributed-cognition work.

It is **not yet project truth**.

## External counterevidence

Dynamic-graph research is a useful warning because it contains a closely related intuition: preserving the `mental map` by changing existing positions as little as possible should help users understand evolving information.

Empirical results have been mixed.

Archambault & Purchase's review reports that simple mental-map preservation did not reliably improve response time/error rate across several dynamic-graph comprehension studies.

Other experiments did find benefits when tasks specifically required **orientation/navigation through sufficiently complex evolving layouts**.

The important lesson is not about graphs themselves.

It is:

> **Spatial stability is task-dependent. It does not earn value merely by minimizing movement.**

## Implication for Tysiac

The hand hypothesis is strongest only if the player's real task uses spatial orientation.

Potential supporting evidence would be:

- recurring personal arrangement;
- faster return to a known card/group after interruption;
- reduced search after card insertion/removal;
- reconstruction of similar layout after disruption;
- deliberate use of gaps/edges as cognitive landmarks.

Without this evidence, preserving positions may simply fossilize a mediocre layout.

## Competing value: semantic reconfiguration

Sometimes moving more is better because a new layout expresses a new situation more clearly.

Examples to test later:

- a new card may deserve a noticeable intake transition rather than stealthy minimum displacement;
- a phase change may justify regrouping if the semantic task genuinely changes;
- an accessibility mode may need stronger spacing/readability even if it disrupts prior pixel positions;
- a small screen/orientation change may require a new geometry rather than preserving a broken one.

Therefore the target should not be:

`minimize total movement`

but something closer to:

> **preserve task-relevant relationships and landmarks unless a reconfiguration creates greater semantic/readability value.**

## Distinguish three stability goals

### Identity stability

`this is the same card/object`

Very strong default.

### Relational stability

`this card remains near/in relation to this group/edge/neighbor`

Potentially high cognitive value, but needs evidence.

### Pixel stability

`this card remains at almost the same coordinates`

Weakest claim. Often unnecessary and sometimes harmful.

The project should prefer identity/relational continuity over literal pixel preservation.

## Challenge to H1 local field

A local field may preserve far landmarks but still be worse if:

- the new gap is harder to read than a crisp slot;
- neighboring motion creates more visual search;
- movement implies physical relationships that do not matter strategically;
- the hand becomes harder to scan by suit/rank;
- global rearrangement would produce a substantially more useful task layout.

H0 must therefore remain a competent baseline.

## Challenge to personal ordering

User-controlled order may not always help.

Potential failure modes:

- novice arrangement is inconsistent and increases search;
- the player wants one-click automatic grouping more than persistence;
- hand changes too rapidly for stable landmarks to matter;
- manual organization becomes busywork;
- cross-device geometry makes literal persistence confusing;
- strategy benefits from a domain-aware sort more than personal topology.

The Owner's strong desire for reorder is sufficient to prioritize the research, not sufficient to prove cognitive benefit.

## Research consequence

Future tests must include **orientation/resumption tasks**, not only subjective preference.

Candidate evidence:

- brief visual interruption, then locate a previously known card/group;
- remove/add one card, then locate an unchanged card;
- compare whole-hand resort versus minimum-relational-disturbance;
- observe whether personal layout is reconstructed after forced reset;
- measure whether stable landmarks reduce search after several real hands rather than after one minute of lab use.

## Donor correction

Do not promote `minimum necessary disturbance` as a universal donor law.

A more defensible candidate is:

> **Relationship-preserving reconfiguration:** preserve identity and task-relevant spatial relationships when they carry learned meaning; allow stronger rearrangement when the semantic/readability benefit clearly outweighs continuity cost.

Status remains **SEED**.
