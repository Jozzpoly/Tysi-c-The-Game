# Owner Mobile Recording 001

Source: Owner-provided mobile screen recording, 2026-09-13.

Technical facts observed from the file:

- duration: ~109.05 s;
- frame size: 1080 × 2400;
- average frame rate: ~51 fps;
- no audio stream in the recording.

This is visual/touch-behavior evidence only. It cannot support claims about audio or haptics.

## What the Owner actually did

The dominant behavior throughout the recording is repeated interaction with the private hand rather than inspection of the scoreboard or static table chrome.

Observed actions include:

- repeated pickup and horizontal reordering of cards;
- repeated lifting of cards above the hand and returning them;
- switching among the prototype's PAPER / WEIGHT / MAGNETIC behavior modes;
- testing the rule-resistance behavior on cards represented as constrained by the fixture;
- adding cards and then continuing to manipulate the expanded hand;
- testing accepted play into the center play region;
- testing rejection/latency controls;
- enabling the prototype sound option (the recording itself contains no audio track, so sound quality is not assessable here);
- returning again to reordering and card manipulation late in the session.

The important evidence is behavioral: the hand itself attracted repeated exploration. The rest of the screen was largely context for that manipulation.

## Positive directional evidence

The prototype crossed the minimum threshold where the Owner naturally wanted to keep touching and moving cards. That validates direct manipulation and persistent personal ordering as research directions.

The WEIGHT-style trailing/resistance behavior is visibly distinct from direct 1:1 tracking in some drags, demonstrating that material character can be varied perceptibly without changing game semantics.

The fixture also exposed the value of reversible local response before authoritative consequence: cards can be manipulated immediately while accepted/rejected outcomes remain separate.

## Material shortcomings visible in the recording

### Finger occlusion and grab geometry

The manipulated card frequently remains under the touch indicator. On a physical phone the finger therefore obscures the object being inspected. The system needs a deliberate relationship between contact point, card pivot and visual lift; simple pointer-following is insufficient.

### Reordering still behaves like slots

Neighbour cards make room, but the hand often reads as discrete slot reassignment rather than a continuously deforming physical group. The dragged card and surrounding cards should participate in one responsive field rather than one object moving over a mostly static row.

### Material modes are still shallow variants

PAPER / WEIGHT / MAGNETIC mostly vary motion/lag/resistance on top of the same interaction skeleton. For donor-quality research, materiality must also influence pickup, pivot, neighbour displacement, release, cancellation, insertion, target attraction, settling and sound/haptic grammar.

### Constraint teaching still leans on copy

The fixture uses labels such as rule resistance / must follow suit as support. The intended future standard is that the constraint is first understandable through behavior and only secondarily explained with local text.

### Commit destination is abstract

Accepted cards move into a generic central region and receive a textual acceptance cue. That does not yet create a rich relationship among hand, play space, opponents, trick ownership and future consequence.

### Resting hand has too little life and structure

The hand is readable but still primarily a row/fan of independent rectangles. It does not yet communicate enough about ownership, grouping, insertion opportunities, focus, recent manipulation or the player's own organizational intent.

## Strong inference, not yet proof

The recording supports treating the hand as the primary interaction surface rather than a secondary HUD element. It does not yet prove the final form of the hand, preferred material model, final drag mechanics, or ideal amount of inertia.

The correct response is not to polish the current fixture. The correct response is to use this recording as baseline evidence for a deeper reset of Card Object and Hand Workspace research.

## Future comparison contract

Later prototypes should be compared against this recording on at least:

- how quickly the Owner starts manipulating cards without instruction;
- whether reordering becomes more exploratory and less corrective;
- whether the finger hides less useful information;
- whether neighbouring cards react continuously and intelligibly;
- whether legal/illegal behavior can be understood before reading text;
- whether latency/rejection feels like coherent physical resistance rather than UI rollback;
- whether the Owner continues touching the hand even when no gameplay progress is required;
- whether the experience remains pleasurable after repeated manipulation rather than only on first contact.
