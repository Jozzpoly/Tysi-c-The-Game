# Experience Research Program

This program exists to prevent the project from collapsing back into fast UI iteration.

The work is divided into evidence stages. Each stage can expand into multiple conversations and fixtures. The stage ends only when further work has diminishing value or a sufficiently grounded hypothesis exists for the next stage.

## Stage 0 — Reset and evidence model

Recover useful facts from previous experiments without inheriting their implementation. Define Owner authority for experience questions, preserve game-truth separation, record the first mobile evidence and establish donor-level quality criteria.

## Stage 1 — Card embodiment

Research pickup, grab point, occlusion, pivot, translation, tilt, inertia, acceleration, drag transfer, cancellation, snapback, release, material variation, edge/face behavior and perceptual object continuity.

Question: can a single card feel like a coherent digital object before it participates in any game rule?

## Stage 2 — Hand as embodied workspace

Research fan/stack geometry, custom ordering, persistent organization, insertion, neighbour displacement, grouping, focus, expansion/compression, newly received cards, removed cards and restoration after rejected actions.

Question: can the hand become a private place the player inhabits and organizes rather than a row of controls?

## Stage 3 — Intent and commit

Separate exploration, pickup, reorder, aim, play intent and irreversible commit without explicit UI modes. Stress accidental input, one-hand use, interrupted gestures and ambiguous diagonal movement.

Question: can the same physical language support both arranging and playing cards without hidden modes or surprise?

## Stage 4 — Self-teaching constraint language

Translate canonical legality into affordance, attraction, resistance, focus, local explanation and failed-attempt recovery. No tutorial arrows as the primary mechanism.

Question: can a new player discover routine legal interaction by manipulating the world?

## Stage 5 — Table topology and opponent presence

Only after Card/Hand is strong: determine what the rest of the game space must become. Research origin/destination, ownership, opponent action presence, spatial causality and attention routing.

Question: can the game feel socially and causally inhabited without skeuomorphic table simulation or avatar theater?

## Stage 6 — Temporal grammar

Research anticipation, commit, impact, consequence, settle, minimum causal hold and cosmetic tail across ordinary repeated actions.

Question: can feedback be rich without making the game slow?

## Stage 7 — Stakes and escalation

Research how contract, trump/marriage, scoring, bomb and match resolution alter the sensory/visual field with increasing significance while preserving normal-play headroom.

Question: does the game have meaningful dynamic range rather than uniform juice?

## Stage 8 — Sound / haptic / motion orchestration

Treat modalities as one causal event, not independent decorations. Preserve silent and reduced-motion comprehension. Haptics remain optional enhancement.

## Stage 9 — Mobile and desktop embodiment

Develop device-specific interaction bodies over shared semantics. Mobile is not a squeezed desktop; desktop is not a mouse-emulated phone.

## Stage 10 — Torture and fatigue

Repeat common actions at scale, inject latency/rejection/reconnect, interrupt attention, vary speed, use large/small hands and test accidental gestures. Novelty is explicitly treated as confounder.

## Stage 11 — First integrated candidate

Only after donor-quality primitives survive earlier stages: build a real presentation candidate over `SeatProjection + GameEvent[]`, preserving authority/privacy and reconnect behavior.

## Evidence discipline

For each experiment record:

- research question;
- what is deliberately held constant;
- variable(s) changed;
- what Owner did, not only what Owner said;
- positive/negative spontaneous comments;
- friction, confusion and ignored affordances;
- novelty/fatigue risk;
- demonstrated result vs inference;
- promotion/rejection decision;
- transferable principle, if any.

## No-fast-forward rule

A later stage may reveal an earlier assumption is wrong. In that case return to the earlier stage. The program is not a checklist to complete; it is a scaffold for deeper evidence.
